Website & service request checklist for local businesses

A useful website does more than show a phone number. Use this checklist to see whether customers can understand your services, explain what they need, and get a clear next step. It works whether you are planning a new site or improving an existing one.

Follow the customer's path

Try each step on a phone, as though you were a first-time customer.

01 · Find the right service

Can visitors tell what you do?

  • Services and service area are clear near the top of the page.
  • Each service has a plain-language description, not just a name.
  • Phone, text, booking, or request buttons are easy to find and tap.
  • Photos and examples reflect work you actually offer and have useful alternative text.

02 · Make a useful request

Can customers send the details you need?

  • Ask only for contact details and job information needed for the first response.
  • Let customers choose a service and describe the issue in their own words.
  • If photos or location details would help, make them optional and explain why.
  • Test the form on mobile and make required fields, errors, and privacy expectations clear.

03 · Close the loop

Does everyone know what happens next?

  • Show a confirmation after a request is submitted.
  • Route the request to the right person or shared inbox.
  • Keep the customer's request and follow-up status together so nothing gets lost.
  • Explain the next step and realistic response expectations without promising an instant reply.

Before you launch or revise

Check the foundations, too.

  • Keep the domain and core business accounts in your business's name.
  • Write a distinct page title and description that accurately describe each important page.
  • Use readable headings and links, and check that forms work with a keyboard.
  • Publish a sitemap, check the site's indexing settings, and test the live links and forms.
  • Review the site as services, hours, photos, and contact details change.

These steps make a site easier to use and easier for search engines to understand. They do not guarantee a search ranking or a number of new leads.
